# More sales. Less margin. Would you run the offer?

**Margin Map helps a store understand its margins before changing prices or promotions.**

## The real starting point

I started exploring dashboards to understand the numbers in my own e-commerce
business. That practical use informed Margin Map's focus on product costs,
bundles and promotions. I did not record a specific campaign result or a measured
financial improvement attributable to the tool.

The public walkthrough below is an **illustrative decision case**, using the
synthetic Luma Vale store. It demonstrates the reasoning the tool supports;
it is not a customer testimonial, a historical campaign or evidence of increased profit.

## The decision

A store is considering a promotion. Effective selling prices would fall 20%,
and the team assumes it could generate 30% more orders. A revenue-only model
looks encouraging: `0.80 × 1.30 = 1.04`, or 4% more sales.

But every additional order still needs products, fulfilment and payment processing.
The decision is whether the extra orders compensate for the margin given away.

## Follow the calculation

[Open the guided demo](https://marginmap.eilvi.com/#decision-case), or open the
local dashboard and choose **Explore the decision**. The two presets
use the complete imported sample period, 22 October 2025–22 October 2026.
The same calculation also works on a selected SKU, promotion or date range in
Scenario lab, where you can edit each assumption.

| Metric | Baseline | 20% lower price, 30% more orders | 10% lower price, 20% more orders |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sales including VAT | €83,755.43 | €87,105.65 | €90,455.86 |
| Product margin | €51,319.39 | €48,718.32 | €53,277.01 |
| After declared variable costs | €35,166.91 | €31,645.84 | €36,444.20 |
| Order growth needed to preserve baseline after-cost margin | — | 40.5% | 16.8% |

The deeper offer raises sales while reducing the result after declared variable
costs by €3,521.07. Its assumed 30% order growth is below the 40.5% recovery
threshold. The smaller offer performs better **under its own unverified demand
assumption**. This does not establish that either demand increase is achievable.

The next commercial step would be a controlled test of demand and acquisition
cost, with a stop condition tied to contribution. No such test or campaign has
been run as part of this public case.

## What stays fixed, and what changes

- Price changes apply to the prices already paid, after existing discounts;
  they are not percentage-point changes to discount codes.
- Basket composition, units per order, VAT treatment and existing discount rates
  remain constant. More orders therefore mean proportionally more units.
- Product costs, fulfilment, shipping charged and fixed payment fees scale with
  volume. The percentage payment fee also responds to selling price.
- Advertising remains at the baseline allocation. Achieving more orders with
  that budget is an assumption to test. Allocated spend is not causal attribution.
- Fixed overhead and new refund behaviour are excluded. Historical order
  adjustments remain in the baseline. The result is not net profit or cash flow.
- Luma Vale's workbook is in Scenario mode: current Catalogue costs form the
  baseline. Actual mode instead applies dated costs, including bundle components.

The figures come from `assets/unit-economics-input.xlsx`, generated by
`src/make_demo_workbook.py --as-of 2026-10-22`, and the shared browser calculation
in `src/scenario_lab.js`. Automated tests derive the case from the generated
dashboard; there is no separate hard-coded calculation behind the presentation.

## Engineering decisions behind the story

**Preserve historical meaning.** Actual mode resolves product costs at the sale
date. A supplier price change does not rewrite earlier dated costs. Shopify's
current cost is recorded from its observation date; the tool asks for earlier
evidence rather than pretending it knows the past. Bundles inherit the effective
costs of their components.

**Make uncertainty visible.** An undeclared product cost blocks the report.
Missing variable costs produce a visibly incomplete known-cost subtotal. Explicit
zero, missing input, declared rate and allocation have different meanings.
Data controls explains the basis of each input and the order-date treatment of refunds.

**Make the work reproducible.** Shopify snapshots or an Excel workbook feed a
validated line-level model, SQLite and a self-contained HTML dashboard plus PDF.
Calculations and exports use the same declared inputs. Cent-level allocations
preserve totals when users filter the report.

**Keep operation small.** The public workflow uses Python's standard library,
SQLite and browser JavaScript. It can produce a local report without a hosted
database or dashboard account. This trades continuous sync and broad integrations
for inspectable calculations and simple operation.

Development includes AI-assisted implementation and review. The evidence for
these decisions is the source, regression tests and reproducible outputs, not
an assertion that the project was written without assistance.

## Evidence and boundaries

- **Real context:** the owner's use of dashboards to understand their own business,
  reported by the owner; no business data is included here.
- **Public demonstration:** synthetic store data and editable commercial assumptions.
- **Technical evidence:** tests cover historical costs, missing inputs, allocations,
  refunds, VAT, the scenario calculation and report reproducibility.
- **Still to validate:** whether the presentation communicates the value to its
  intended audience, and whether an actual promotion achieves its assumed demand.
- **Release evidence:** version 1.3.0 and its automated checks are available on
  [GitHub](https://github.com/eduardosalomonm/margin-map/releases/tag/v1.3.0).
  Publication is not evidence of a real-store Shopify pilot or commercial uplift.

## Reproduce locally

```bash
python3 src/run_excel_dashboard.py --output output/dashboard.html --open
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
node --test tests/test_*.js
```

Select **Explore the decision**, inspect the 20% offer, try the 10% alternative,
then change the volume assumption. Use **Data controls → Input assumptions**
to inspect what the calculation knows and what it assumes.
